# mini-soldat

A clone of [Soldat](https://github.com/opensoldat/opensoldat) (Michał
Marcinkowski, 2002) written from scratch in OCaml: a side-view
deathmatch, soldiers running, jumping and flying on jet boots over a
map of polygons, shooting and throwing grenades, and falling as
ragdolls.

It started as [elm-playground](https://github.com/aryx/ocaml-elm-playground)'s
TinySoldat, a toy of 600 lines: one screen of flat-coloured polygons,
you against two bots, the first to 5 kills. Here it grows, towards the
real thing: Soldat's maps, textures, soldier, weapons and feel, from
its own sources and content, both public now (see
[docs/opensoldat.md](docs/opensoldat.md)). The way there: Soldat's
Pascal adapted to OCaml and to the Playground's API and physics, in far
less code, with the same feel in the hands.

Where it is ([docs/plan.md](docs/plan.md) has the steps): the soldier
moves as Soldat's does, by its rules and its numbers, ported from its
Pascal: the run, the wind-up before a jump, the jump sideways, the
crouch, lying down and crawling, the roll, the backflip, the jets and
their fuel, ice. And it looks as Soldat's does: its pictures, one on
each limb of its skeleton, in its colours, played by Soldat's own 44
animations, its head and its arms turned to the cursor; dead, the same
body let loose. It runs on Soldat's own maps: any of its 99
`.pms` files is read and played on, and drawn as Soldat draws it: its
texture on its polygons, shaded by their corners, and its scenery,
through Soldat's camera. And it fights with Soldat's weapons, their
numbers read from its own `weapons.ini`: the ten primaries and the
USSOCOM, the clip and the reload, the two Eagles, the shotgun's
pellets and kick, the Barrett's wait, grenades thrown harder the
longer held, the M79; bullets that ricochet, go through a body, lose
strength with distance; explosions that push, and take a head or five
limbs off. Its bots are still the toy's.

## Building

It stands on elm-playground's packages, 0.3.3 or later: the Playground
for its window, its drawing and its physics, on one of its two native
platforms (SDL for the window either way, and Cairo,
`elm_playground_native`, or the Playground's own rasterizer,
`elm_playground_software`) or in a browser (`elm_playground_web`), and
`tiny_libs` for the 2D physics underneath, the bots' senses and the
server's networking.

```bash
./configure    # the opam dependencies, and checks for SDL2 and Cairo
make
make test
```

`./configure --software` leaves Cairo out altogether, as it does by
itself when Cairo is not found.

Working on both repositories side by side, install elm-playground's
packages from its checkout into your switch instead (again whenever
mini-soldat should see a change there), then build here:

```bash
(cd ../ocaml-elm-playground && make && make install)
./configure
make
```

Then `./bin/mini-soldat` (a symlink into `_build`, alive after a
`make`), or `make run`: drawn by Cairo when its platform is installed,
else by the Playground's own rasterizer. `./bin/mini-soldat-software`
(`make run-software`) is always the latter.

## In a browser

The same game is compiled to JavaScript by js_of_ocaml, on the
Playground's web platform (`elm_playground_web`: its shapes drawn as
SVG). `make serve` builds it and serves it at http://localhost:8001/;
its flags go after a `?` (`index.html?hitboxes&ai=engine`). It wants
elm-playground after 0.3.5 to run well there: before, its web platform
kept 32 pictures and the textured map with its soldiers is 64
(`docs/architecture.md`).

The website is `docs/`, what GitHub Pages serves: a page written by
hand (`docs/index.html`), the game (`docs/play.html`,
`docs/MiniSoldat.bc.js`) and the content it fetches (`docs/assets/`:
Arena2's texture and scenery, as plain pixels), copied there by `make
website` (to commit after; `make serve-website` to look at it first, at
http://localhost:8000/). Once Pages is enabled for the repository
("Deploy from a branch", `/docs`), it is at
https://aryx.github.io/mini-soldat/.

## The server

`./bin/mini-soldat-server` is where players will meet to play over the
network. For now it is a lobby: players connect (WebSocket, so a
browser can), name themselves, enter rooms and talk; the games played
in the rooms are to come ([docs/network.md](docs/network.md)).

```bash
./bin/mini-soldat-server                       # 127.0.0.1:23073, this computer only
./bin/mini-soldat-server bind=0.0.0.0 port=23073 capacity=32
```

Nothing in the game talks to it yet: its tests do (`tests/server`).

## Playing

Soldat's keys:

```
a/d    run             w   jump           s   crouch
x      lie down, and get up               mouse: aim
left button: shoot     right button (or shift): the jets
r      reload          q   the other weapon
e      a grenade: held longer, thrown harder
1-9, 0 the weapon to appear with (Soldat's menu)
```

and its moves: down and a side while running is a roll; up and a side
a jump sideways; the jets' key during a jump sideways, with the other
side held, a backflip; lying, a side crawls. Running away from the
cursor is running backwards: a soldier faces where it aims.

The key `g` goes round the graphics, the game as each step of its
making drew it: 1 the soldiers as skeletons on flat colours, 2 their
pictures, 3 the map's texture and scenery.

Flags: without any, the game is on Arena2, which the program carries,
its texture and scenery in `data/` (run it from the repository; without
them the map is in flat colours). With a checkout of
[opensoldat/base](https://github.com/opensoldat/base), all of Soldat's
99 maps: `./bin/mini-soldat base=~/opensoldat-base/shared map=ctf_Ash`
(`base=` where the content is, `map=` a map's name, or a `.pms` file).
`graphics=N` starts at that way of drawing, `weapon=N` with that
weapon (its key in the menu: 1 the Desert Eagles ... 0 the minigun).
`hitboxes` draws the points the game tests (the particle, the head and
the feet, the circles a bullet hits), `sticks` the soldiers' skeletons
over them; `ai=engine` puts the bots on
elm-playground's `Sense` and `Bot`
(they know only what they have seen, and react as late as a hand does)
instead of the ones written by hand.

## Layout

Each folder a library, in the order they depend on each other, after
Soldat's own split between what the game is (`shared/`) and what shows
it (`client/`):

```
src/map/      the map: one of Soldat's .pms files read (Pms), the map
              the game plays on made of it: its walls with their perps,
              found by its sectors, what stops a soldier and a bullet,
              its spawn points, what is in sight from where
              (Soldat_map); each texture's mean colour (Texture_tints)
src/anim/     the soldier's animations and skeleton: their files read
              (Poa), the 44 animations with their speeds, carried in
              the program (Soldat_anims; Gen_anims packs them at build
              time)
src/game/     the game without its picture: the weapons' numbers, read
              from Soldat's weapons.ini (Soldat_weapons); a soldier
              that moves and fires as Soldat's, its particle, its keys,
              its collision with the map, its guns (Soldat_soldier);
              dead its skeleton let loose, and what a hard hit cuts of
              it (Soldat_ragdoll); the state (Soldat_model); the
              bullets, what they hit, the explosions (Soldat_bullets);
              the bots (Soldat_bots); a tick and the rounds
              (Soldat_update)
src/assets/   Soldat's content got while the game runs, from a folder
              or the web (Soldat_assets); a .bmp read (Bmp); Gen_assets
              turns pictures into pixels for the website
src/render/   the picture of a tick, as shapes (Soldat_view); the
              soldier as Soldat draws it, a picture on each limb, its
              weapons in its hands and on its back
              (Soldat_gostek; Gen_pictures packs the pictures at build
              time); the map as Soldat draws it, in tiles (Soldat_scene)
              drawn by hand, textured triangles and sprites
              (Soldat_raster)
src/main/     the main: the Playground run (MiniSoldat.ml); software/
              and web/ the same source on the software platform and in
              a browser (web/index.html its page)
src/net/      what a player's program and the server say to each other,
              as bytes (Soldat_protocol)
src/server/   the server: who is in which room, as a value
              (Soldat_lobby), the sockets around it (Soldat_server),
              and main/ its program (MiniSoldatServer.ml)
tests/        map: Arena2 read and made into the game's; anim: the
              animations' files and frames; assets: a .bmp, a picture
              as pixels; render: where a soldier's parts go, a triangle
              and a sprite drawn; game: the soldier's moves and its
              weapons against numbers worked out from Soldat's, and
              rounds played without drawing them; server: the protocol's
              bytes, the lobby's rule, the server over localhost
data/         Soldat's own content copied here (CC BY 4.0, not LGPL:
              data/README.md): maps/Arena2.pms, the map carried in the
              program; anims/ and objects/gostek.po, the soldier's
              animations and skeleton; gostek-gfx/, its pictures;
              weapons.ini, the weapons' numbers; weapons-gfx/, their
              pictures; textures/ and scenery-gfx/, Arena2's; the last
              three read when the game runs
scripts/      build/: a file as an OCaml module holding it in base64;
              assets/: the textures' mean colours, from a checkout of
              the content; perf/: a frame's time, on any map
docs/         the website (index.html, written by hand; play.html and
              MiniSoldat.bc.js, copied by 'make website');
              architecture.md: the program's shape, from a file of
              Soldat's to a pixel on the screen, and what each layer
              keeps;
              opensoldat.md: Soldat's sources and content, what is in
              them, and which of its files each module here stands for;
              network.md: playing over the network, what is there and
              what is to come; notes_soldat_in_playground.md: each of
              Soldat's terms, ideas and pieces of code, and what it is
              here; plan.md: the steps from here to Soldat
```
